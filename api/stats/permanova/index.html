    PERMANOVA and dispersion

    This submodule tests whether groups of samples differ in gene content, using Jaccard distance (1 − Jaccard similarity, which ignores shared absences). The two functions are the PAV equivalents of vegan::adonis2 and vegan::betadisper with vegan::permutest.

    PERMANOVA asks whether group centroids differ more than expected by chance. It is also sensitive to differences in spread, so a significant PERMANOVA should be read together with the dispersion test: if dispersions differ, the PERMANOVA result may reflect spread rather than location.

    To show both on one figure, pass the results to pcoa.centroids:

    import pavvis.plots.pcoa as pcoa

pcoa.centroids(pm, "clade", betadisper_result=bd, permanova_result=pa)

    The plot reuses the results rather than re-running the tests, so the figure annotation matches the reported values.

    PERMANOVA

    Function

    permanova 

    permanova(pm, column, permutations=999, random_state=None)

    PERMANOVA on Jaccard distance for the groups of a discrete metadata column.

    Equivalent to vegan::adonis2(dist ~ group, permutations=...) with Jaccard distance (1 − Jaccard similarity, from pm.compute_jaccard()). The pseudo-F is the ratio of between-group to within-group sums of squared distances, each divided by its degrees of freedom. Significance comes from permuting sample labels: p is the fraction of permutations with a pseudo-F at least as large as observed, with the observed value counted once.

    PERMANOVA is sensitive to differences in dispersion as well as location. Check betadisper before interpreting a significant result as a shift in centroids.

    Example

    from pavvis import PavMatrix
from pavvis.stats.permanova import permanova

pm = PavMatrix("path/to/pav.csv", "path/to/metadata.csv")
pa = permanova(pm, "clade", permutations=9999, random_state=42)
pa

                Df  SumOfSqs      R2        F  Pr(>F)
Model        7   20.1471  0.0859  13.8202  0.0001
Residual  1030  214.5040  0.9141      NaN     NaN
Total     1037  234.6511  1.0000      NaN     NaN

    Interpretation

    R2 is the fraction of the total variation in Jaccard distance explained by the grouping. In the example, clade explains 8.6% of the variation in gene content. Small R² values are typical for PAV data, because much of the variable gene content differs between individual accessions.

    Dispersion (PERMDISP)

    Function

    betadisper 

    betadisper(pm, column, type='median', permutations=999, random_state=None)

    Multivariate homogeneity of group dispersions (PERMDISP) on Jaccard distance.

    Follows vegan::betadisper and vegan::permutest. Samples are placed in PCoA space using all axes, including those with negative eigenvalues (Jaccard distance is not Euclidean). Each group's centre is computed separately in the positive and negative axis spaces, and a sample's distance to its group centre is z = sqrt(d₊² − d₋²), with negative values of d₊² − d₋² set to zero as in vegan. Dispersions are compared with a one-way ANOVA F on z; significance comes from refitting the ANOVA to permuted residuals (z minus its group mean), keeping the group centres fixed.

    Validated against vegan 2.7.5 on the Amborella PAV data: distances and F match to floating-point precision with type='centroid'. With type='median', distances differ by ~1e-5 because vegan stops its BFGS search for the spatial median earlier than the Weiszfeld iteration used here.

    A significant result means the groups differ in spread. In that case a significant PERMANOVA cannot be read purely as a difference in location.

    Example

    from pavvis.stats.permanova import betadisper

bd = betadisper(pm, "clade", permutations=9999, random_state=42)
bd["F"], bd["p_value"]
bd["distances"].groupby(pm.metadata["clade"]).mean()   # mean distance to centre per clade

    Interpretation

    A significant result means some groups are more variable in gene content than others. Use pcoa.dispersion_boxplot(pm, "clade", betadisper_result=bd) to see which.
